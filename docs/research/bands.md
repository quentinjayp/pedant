# Research: the bands

Research for [the levels and scenarios plan](../plans/levels-and-scenarios.md): task 1, the band
descriptors, and task 2, the oral exams' task shapes (in
[Oral exam task shapes](#oral-exam-task-shapes-task-2)). Tasks 3 and 4 (topics, test labels)
will add their own sections.

## Source

Council of Europe (2020), *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching,
assessment – Companion volume*, Council of Europe Publishing, Strasbourg. ISBN 978-92-871-8621-8.
It updates the 2001 CEFR's descriptors and adds scales for mediation and online interaction.

- **Official page:** https://www.coe.int/lang-cefr ("CEFR - Companion volume (2020)").
- **Copy read for this research:** the official site blocks automated downloads, so the copy read was
  the April 2020 edition hosted by blogs.sch.gr (same ISBN):
  https://blogs.sch.gr/mtzotzou/files/2023/09/CEFR-COMPANION-VOLUME_ENGLISH_2020.pdf

Quotations below are the Companion Volume's wording. Page numbers are its own. Every scale that
concerns speaking was read: chapter 3's production, interaction and mediation scales (pages 62–122),
chapter 4's plurilingual repertoire (page 127), chapter 5's linguistic, sociolinguistic and pragmatic
competences (pages 130–142), and Appendix 3 (pages 183–185).

Most scales also have "plus" levels (A2+, B1+, B2+) printed within the level above them. They're
counted with their level here, which makes no difference at band level.

The Companion Volume also has a Pre-A1 level, below the bands, which isn't used here.

## The scales that concern Pedant

The Companion Volume describes each level with many scales. The ones about speaking are these.

**Oral production** (one person speaking at length; pages 62–65):
- Overall oral production
- Sustained monologue: describing experience
- Sustained monologue: giving information
- Sustained monologue: putting a case (e.g. in a debate)
- Addressing audiences
- Public announcements

**Oral interaction** (two or more people talking; pages 72–82):
- Overall oral interaction
- Understanding an interlocutor
- Conversation
- Informal discussion (with friends)
- Formal discussion (meetings)
- Goal-oriented co-operation (cooking together, discussing a document, organising an event, etc.)
- Obtaining goods and services
- Information exchange
- Interviewing and being interviewed
- Using telecommunications

**Interaction strategies:** taking the floor (turntaking), co-operating, and asking for clarification.

**Mediation:** passing meaning between people or texts. It has three groups of scales: mediating a
text, mediating concepts and mediating communication.

**Qualitative features of spoken language** (Appendix 3, pages 183–185): how a speaker at each level
sounds, in six aspects: range, accuracy, fluency, interaction, coherence and phonology. These matter
twice for Pedant. They describe how Gemma should speak at each band, and what the band evals
(task 5) can measure.

## The bands

Each band is two CEFR levels. For each level, the overall scales say what a speaker can do, the
qualitative features say how they sound, and a list of can-do tasks reduces both to things a
conversation can ask for. The task lists are derived for Pedant from the descriptors, and aren't the
Companion Volume's wording.

### Basic (A1–A2)

**Overall oral interaction** (page 72):
- **A1:** "Can interact in a simple way but communication is totally dependent on repetition at a
  slower rate, rephrasing and repair. Can ask and answer simple questions, initiate and respond to
  simple statements in areas of immediate need or on very familiar topics."
- **A2:** "Can interact with reasonable ease in structured situations and short conversations,
  provided the other person helps if necessary. Can manage simple, routine exchanges without undue
  effort; can ask and answer questions and exchange ideas and information on familiar topics in
  predictable everyday situations." Also: "Can handle very short social exchanges but is rarely able
  to understand enough to keep conversation going of their own accord."

**Overall oral production** (page 62):
- **A1:** "Can produce simple, mainly isolated phrases about people and places."
- **A2:** "Can give a simple description or presentation of people, living or working conditions,
  daily routines. likes/dislikes, etc. as a short series of simple phrases and sentences linked into a
  list."

**How they sound** (Appendix 3):
- **Range:**
  - **A1:** "Has a very basic repertoire of words/signs and simple phrases related to personal
    details and particular concrete situations."
  - **A2:** "Uses basic sentence patterns with memorised phrases, groups of a few words/signs and
    formulae in order to communicate limited information in simple everyday situations."
- **Accuracy:**
  - **A1:** "Shows only limited control of a few simple grammatical structures and sentence patterns
    in a memorised repertoire."
  - **A2:** "Uses some simple structures correctly, but still systematically makes basic mistakes."
- **Fluency:**
  - **A1:** "Can manage very short, isolated, mainly pre-packaged utterances, with much pausing to
    search for expressions, to articulate less familiar words, and to repair communication."
  - **A2:** "Can make themselves understood in very short utterances, even though pauses, false
    starts and reformulation are very evident."
- **Interaction:**
  - **A1:** "Can ask and answer questions about personal details."
  - **A2:** "Can ask and answer questions and respond to simple statements."
- **Coherence:**
  - **A1:** links words "with very basic linear connectors like 'and' or 'then'."
  - **A2:** "Can link groups of words with simple connectors like 'and', 'but' and 'because'."
- **Phonology:**
  - **A1:** "Can reproduce correctly a limited range of sounds as well as stress on simple, familiar
    words and phrases."
  - **A2:** "Pronunciation is generally clear enough to be understood, but conversational partners
    will need to ask for repetition from time to time." Also: "Nevertheless, pronunciation of familiar
    words is clear."

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Greet, introduce yourself, ask how someone is, say goodbye.
- Answer and ask questions about yourself, your family, your home, your work and your free time.
- Order a meal; buy something, saying what you want and asking the price; handle numbers, prices and
  times.
- Point out when something is wrong ("The food is cold", "There is no light in my room").
- Get simple information about travel; use public transport; buy tickets; ask for and give directions.
- Make an appointment with a doctor, and say what the problem is in simple terms (a cold, the flu).
- Make and respond to invitations, suggestions and apologies; ask for or offer a favour.
- Make arrangements: when and where to meet, what to do next. Agree and disagree with others.
- Say what you like and don't like, and why you prefer one thing to another.
- Describe your day, your town, a person, in a few simple sentences.
- Ask and answer simple questions about an event: where and when, who was there, what it was like.
- Describe plans for the weekend or the holidays.
- Have a short, simple phone conversation with someone you know on a predictable topic (arrival
  times, arrangements to meet).
- Say you're following, say you didn't follow, or ask someone to repeat or speak more slowly.

### Independent (B1–B2)

**Overall oral interaction** (page 72):
- **B1:** "Can exploit a wide range of simple language to deal with most situations likely to arise
  while travelling. Can enter unprepared into conversation on familiar topics, and express personal
  opinions and exchange information on topics that are familiar, of personal interest or pertinent to
  everyday life (e.g. family, hobbies, work, travel and current events)."
- **B2:** "Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction, and
  sustained relationships with users of the target language, quite possible without imposing strain
  on either party. Can highlight the personal significance of events and experiences, and account for
  and sustain views clearly by providing relevant explanations and arguments." Also: "Can exchange,
  check and confirm information, deal with less routine situations and explain why something is a
  problem."

**Overall oral production** (page 62):
- **B1:** "Can reasonably fluently sustain a straightforward description of one of a variety of
  subjects within their field of interest, presenting it as a linear sequence of points."
- **B2:** "Can give clear, systematically developed descriptions and presentations, with appropriate
  highlighting of significant points, and relevant supporting detail."

**How they sound** (Appendix 3):
- **Range:**
  - **B1:** "Has enough language to get by, with sufficient vocabulary to express themselves with
    some hesitation and circumlocutions on topics such as family, hobbies and interests, work, travel
    and current events."
  - **B2:** "Has a sufficient range of language to give clear descriptions and express viewpoints on
    most general topics, without much conspicuous searching for words, using some complex sentence
    forms to do so."
- **Accuracy:**
  - **B1:** "Uses reasonably accurately a repertoire of frequently used 'routines' and patterns
    associated with more predictable situations."
  - **B2:** "Shows a relatively high degree of grammatical control. Does not make errors that cause
    misunderstanding, and can correct most of their mistakes."
- **Fluency:**
  - **B1:** "Can keep going comprehensibly, even though pausing for grammatical and lexical planning
    and repair is very evident, especially in longer stretches of free production."
  - **B2:** "Can produce stretches of language with a fairly even tempo; although they can be hesitant
    as they search for patterns and expressions, there are few noticeably long pauses."
- **Interaction:**
  - **B1:** "Can initiate, maintain and close simple face-to-face conversation on topics that are
    familiar or of personal interest. Can repeat part of what someone has said to confirm mutual
    understanding."
  - **B2:** "Can initiate discourse, take their turn when appropriate and end conversation when they
    need to, though they may not always do this elegantly. Can help the discussion along on familiar
    ground confirming comprehension, inviting others in, etc."
- **Coherence:**
  - **B1:** "Can link a series of shorter, discrete simple elements into a connected, linear sequence
    of points."
  - **B2:** "Can use a limited number of cohesive devices to link their utterances into clear,
    coherent discourse, though there may be some 'jumpiness' in a long contribution."
- **Phonology:**
  - **B1:** "Pronunciation is generally intelligible; can approximate intonation and stress at both
    utterance and word levels. However, accent is usually influenced by the other language(s) they
    speak."
  - **B2:** "Can generally use appropriate intonation, place stress correctly and articulate
    individual sounds clearly; accent tends to be influenced by the other language(s) they speak, but
    has little or no effect on intelligibility."

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Tell a story about something that happened to you, with how you felt about it.
- Relate the plot of a book or film, and say what you thought of it.
- Describe your plans, hopes and ambitions, with reasons.
- Give your opinion on a familiar topic and explain why; express beliefs, agreement and disagreement
  politely.
- Discuss what to do, where to go, who or which to choose; compare and contrast the options.
- Deal with most situations while travelling or arranging travel: an agent, a tourist office, asking a
  passenger where to get off.
- Cope with less routine situations in shops and banks: ask the difference between two products,
  return an unsatisfactory purchase, make a complaint.
- Follow a complaint up: explain the problem and make it clear the other side must make a concession;
  ask detailed questions about a more complex service (a rental agreement).
- Describe symptoms to a doctor and ask for advice.
- Take part in an interview (a job interview, a consultation), taking some of the initiative; carry
  one out, departing from prepared questions and probing interesting replies (B2).
- Give important details over the phone about an unexpected incident (a problem at a hotel, with a hire
  car).
- Summarise a film, article or talk and give your opinion of it.
- Explain why something is a problem; speculate about causes and consequences; make and respond to
  hypotheses.
- Explain how to do something, giving detailed instructions.
- Pass on to a friend what someone said, in the other language, in an everyday situation.
- Ask follow-up questions, check you've understood, invite the other person in, and keep the
  conversation going.

### Proficient (C1–C2)

**Overall oral interaction** (page 72):
- **C1:** "Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously, almost effortlessly. Has a good command
  of a broad lexical repertoire allowing gaps to be readily overcome with circumlocutions." Also:
  "Can communicate spontaneously with good grammatical control without much sign of having to restrict
  what they want to say, adopting a level of formality appropriate to the circumstances."
- **C2:** "Has a good command of idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms with awareness of
  connotative levels of meaning. Can convey finer shades of meaning precisely by using, with reasonable
  accuracy, a wide range of modification devices. Can backtrack and restructure around a difficulty so
  smoothly that the interlocutor is hardly aware of it."

**Overall oral production** (page 62):
- **C1:** "Can give clear, detailed descriptions and presentations on complex subjects, integrating
  sub-themes, developing particular points and rounding off with an appropriate conclusion."
- **C2:** "Can produce clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured discourse with an effective logical
  structure which helps the recipient to notice and remember significant points."

**How they sound** (Appendix 3):
- **Range:**
  - **C1:** "Has a good command of a broad range of language allowing them to select a formulation to
    express themselves clearly in an appropriate style on a wide range of general, academic,
    professional or leisure topics without having to restrict what they want to say."
  - **C2:** "Shows great flexibility reformulating ideas in differing linguistic forms to convey finer
    shades of meaning precisely, to give emphasis, to differentiate and to eliminate ambiguity. Also
    has a good command of idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms."
- **Accuracy:**
  - **C1:** "Consistently maintains a high degree of grammatical accuracy; errors are rare, difficult
    to spot and generally corrected when they do occur."
  - **C2:** "Maintains consistent grammatical control of complex language, even while attention is
    otherwise engaged (e.g. in forward planning, in monitoring others' reactions)."
- **Fluency:**
  - **C1:** "Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously, almost effortlessly. Only a
    conceptually difficult subject can hinder a natural, smooth flow of language."
  - **C2:** "Can express themselves spontaneously at length with a natural colloquial flow, avoiding
    or backtracking around any difficulty so smoothly that the interlocutor is hardly aware of it."
- **Interaction:**
  - **C1:** "Can select a suitable phrase from a readily available range of discourse functions to
    preface their remarks in order to get or to keep the floor and to relate their own contributions
    skilfully to those of other speakers."
  - **C2:** "Can interact with ease and skill, picking up and using non-verbal and intonational cues
    apparently effortlessly. Can interweave their contribution into the joint discourse with fully
    natural turntaking, referencing, allusion making, etc."
- **Coherence:**
  - **C1:** "Can produce clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured language, showing controlled use of
    organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices."
  - **C2:** "Can create coherent and cohesive discourse making full and appropriate use of a variety
    of organisational patterns and a wide range of connectors and other cohesive devices."
- **Phonology:**
  - **C1:** "Can employ the full range of phonological features in the target language with
    sufficient control to ensure intelligibility throughout." Also: "some features of accent retained
    from other language(s) may be noticeable, but they do not affect intelligibility at all."
  - **C2:** the full range of phonological features "with a high level of control – including prosodic
    features such as word and sentence stress, rhythm and intonation – so that the finer points of
    their message are clear and precise."

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Persuade someone to change their mind, without offending them.
- Indicate reservations and reluctance, state conditions when agreeing, and ask for understanding of
  your position.
- Advise on or discuss a sensitive issue without awkwardness; frame critical remarks or strong
  disagreement diplomatically.
- Smooth over a conflict between two other people: find what each side needs and what it might give up,
  and suggest a way forward.
- Explain something complex (a decision, a system, a situation) clearly and in structure, or present
  it to someone unfamiliar with it.
- Argue a formal position and answer counter-arguments, even hostile questioning, on the spot.
- Shift register: the same request to a friend, a stranger and a boss, and keep it consistent.
- Use and understand emotional, allusive and joking language, humour and irony.
- Summarise and weigh up several viewpoints in a discussion, and draw a conclusion.
- Negotiate a dispute or a sensitive transaction: who pays for damage, who is to blame.
- Interpret between two people informally, explaining cultural references.

Plain exchanges of facts aren't Proficient tasks: the information exchange scale has no C-level
descriptors, "because merely exchanging factual information is no longer a main focus in learning
objectives for proficient users" (page 78).

## Strategies

The strategy scales describe how a speaker keeps a conversation working when language runs short.
They matter for how Gemma reacts to the learner, more than for what scenarios ask.

**Asking for clarification** (page 89):
- **A1:** "Can express in a simple way that they do not understand."
- **A2:** "Can ask very simply for repetition when they do not understand." Also asks about key words
  "using stock phrases".
- **B1–B2:** "Can ask someone to clarify or elaborate what they have just said"; asks follow-up
  questions to check what someone meant.
- **C1:** asks for clarification of "complex, abstract ideas in professional or academic contexts".

**Compensating** (page 69), when a word won't come:
- **A levels:** pointing, gestures, "an inadequate word/sign from their repertoire".
- **B1:** "Can use a simple word/sign meaning something similar to the concept they want to convey and
  invite 'correction'." Also: "Can 'foreignise' word/signs in their first language and ask for
  confirmation."
- **B2:** "Can use circumlocution and paraphrase to cover gaps in vocabulary and structure."
- **C2:** "Can substitute an equivalent term for a word/sign they can't recall, so smoothly that it is
  scarcely noticeable."

**Building on plurilingual repertoire** (page 127): the CEFR counts using words from another language
as a skill at every level, not a failure. At the A levels, the learner uses "all possible resources in
order to handle a simple everyday transaction"; from B2, they alternate between languages flexibly to
help communication. The roadmap's "Reaching for a missing word" (say the sentence with one word in
your own language) is this, and the B1 compensating descriptor.

**Turntaking and co-operating** (page 88): from A2 "Can initiate, maintain and close simple,
face-to-face conversation" and "Can indicate when they are following", through B1 repeating back
"part of what someone has said to confirm mutual understanding", to C1 relating "own contribution
skilfully to that of others".

## Mediation

Mediation is passing meaning on: between two people, from a text, or between languages
(pages 90–122). Two scales fit a conversation app:

- **Acting as an intermediary in informal situations** (page 115): passing on what one person said to
  another who doesn't share their language. It runs from A1 ("other people's personal details and very
  simple, predictable information … provided other people help with formulation"), through B1 (the main
  sense on subjects of personal interest), to C2 (conveying "finer shades of meaning and elaborating on
  sociocultural implications").
- **Facilitating communication in delicate situations and disagreements** (page 116): at the A levels
  the speaker can recognise when people disagree and use simple phrases to show sympathy or seek
  compromise; at B2 they "outline the main points in a disagreement … and explain the positions of the
  parties involved"; at C1 they "formulate a diplomatic request to each side … to determine what is
  central to their position, and what they may be willing to give up".

The text-based mediation scales (relaying information from a text, explaining data, processing text,
note-taking, responding to literature) need a text, which Pedant doesn't show.

## Phonology

The Companion Volume's phonological control scale (pages 133–135) replaced the 2001 one, because "the
progression appeared unrealistic" and its focus on "accent and on accuracy instead of on
intelligibility has been detrimental to the development of the teaching of pronunciation". The new
scale has three parts: overall phonological control, sound articulation, and prosodic features
(stress, rhythm, intonation). "Intelligibility has been a key factor in discriminating between levels.
The focus is on how much effort is required from the interlocutor to decode the speaker's message."

**Sound articulation** (pages 134–135):
- **A1:** "Can reproduce sounds in the target language if carefully guided. Can articulate a limited
  number of sounds, so that speech is only intelligible if the interlocutor provides support (e.g. by
  repeating correctly and by eliciting repetition of new sounds)."
- **A2:** "Systematic mispronunciation of phonemes does not hinder intelligibility, provided the
  interlocutor makes an effort to recognise and adjust to the influence of the speaker's language
  background on pronunciation."
- **B1:** "Is generally intelligible throughout, despite regular mispronunciation of individual sounds
  and words they are less familiar with."
- **B2:** "Can articulate a high proportion of the sounds in the target language clearly in extended
  stretches of production; is intelligible throughout, despite a few systematic mispronunciations."
- **C1:** "Can articulate virtually all the sounds of the target language with a high degree of
  control. They can usually self-correct if they noticeably mispronounce a sound."
- **C2:** "Can articulate virtually all the sounds of the target language with clarity and precision."

**Prosodic features** (pages 134–135):
- **A1:** "Can use the prosodic features of a limited repertoire of simple words and phrases
  intelligibly, in spite of a very strong influence on stress, rhythm and/or intonation from the other
  language(s) they speak; their interlocutor needs to be collaborative."
- **A2:** "Prosodic features (e.g. word stress) are adequate for familiar everyday words and simple
  utterances."
- **B1:** "Can convey their message in an intelligible way in spite of a strong influence on stress,
  intonation and/or rhythm from the other language(s) they speak."
- **B2:** "Can employ prosodic features (e.g. stress, intonation, rhythm) to support the message they
  intend to convey, though with some influence from the other languages they speak."
- **C1:** "Can vary intonation and place stress correctly in order to express precisely what they mean
  to say."
- **C2:** "Can exploit prosodic features (e.g. stress, rhythm and intonation) appropriately and
  effectively in order to convey finer shades of meaning (e.g. to differentiate and emphasise)."

**For Pedant:**
- **The standard differs, by design.** The CEFR measures how easily a listener understands; Pedant
  grades how close each sound is to one voice's pronunciation, a clear model to learn from. What the
  CEFR's scales can inform is how lenient grading is at each band: systematic mispronunciation is
  expected at the A levels and B1.
- **Prosody isn't graded.** Pedant grades sounds, not stress, rhythm or intonation, which the CEFR
  treats as half of phonological control from A1. (The roadmap's "Tones and intonation" item would add
  some of it.)
- **What fits Pedant's approach:** A1's "can reproduce sounds … if carefully guided", with the
  interlocutor "repeating correctly and … eliciting repetition of new sounds", is close to what
  Repeat word does.

## Register

**Sociolinguistic appropriateness** (pages 136–137) is where the CEFR describes register:
- **A1–A2:** polite forms of greeting and address; "Can make and respond to invitations, suggestions,
  apologies, etc."
- **B1:** "Can perform and respond to a wide range of language functions, using their most common
  exponents in a neutral register." Also: "Is aware of the salient politeness conventions and acts
  appropriately."
- **B2:** "Can express themselves confidently, clearly and politely in a formal or informal register,
  appropriate to the situation and person(s) concerned." Also: "Can adjust their expression to make
  some distinction between formal and informal registers but may not always do so appropriately."
- **C1:** "Can adjust their level of formality (register and style) to suit the social context: formal,
  informal or colloquial as appropriate, and maintain a consistent register." Also: "Can frame critical
  remarks or express strong disagreement diplomatically."
- **C2:** "Has a good command of idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms with awareness of connotative
  levels of meaning."

**For Pedant:** the CEFR expects a neutral register at the A levels and B1, choosing between formal
and informal from B2, and consistent control of formal, informal and colloquial at C1. Pedant's
Formal / Casual setting asks for a register at every band.

## Vocabulary and grammar

**Vocabulary range** (page 131):
- **A1:** "Has a basic vocabulary repertoire of words/signs and phrases related to particular concrete
  situations."
- **A2:** "Has sufficient vocabulary to conduct routine everyday transactions involving familiar
  situations and topics."
- **B1:** "Has sufficient vocabulary to express themselves with some circumlocutions on most topics
  pertinent to their everyday life such as family, hobbies and interests, work, travel and current
  events."
- **B2:** "Has a good range of vocabulary for matters connected to their field and most general topics.
  Can vary formulation to avoid frequent repetition, but lexical gaps can still cause hesitation and
  circumlocution."
- **C1:** "Has a good command of a broad lexical repertoire allowing gaps to be readily overcome with
  circumlocutions"; "Has a good command of common idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms; can play
  with words/signs fairly well."
- **C2:** "Has a good command of a very broad lexical repertoire including idiomatic expressions and
  colloquialisms; shows awareness of connotative levels of meaning."

**Grammatical accuracy** (page 132) follows the qualitative features above, with one note: "research
in English, French and German suggests that inaccuracy increases at around B1 as the learner is
beginning to use language more independently and creatively." Accuracy doesn't rise in a straight line.

## Production beyond conversation

- **Addressing audiences** (page 65): a prepared presentation, then questions. From B1 ("a prepared,
  straightforward presentation on a familiar topic within their field") to C2 ("Can handle difficult and even hostile
  questioning"). Possible as a Free talk monologue followed by Gemma's questions.
- **Public announcements** (pages 64–65): short rehearsed announcements, A2 to C1. Nothing to
  interact with.

## Which descriptors Pedant can exercise

Pedant is a spoken conversation with Gemma, one turn at a time. The learner's speech is heard by a
speech recognizer and ZIPA.

**What fits:**
- **Interaction (conversation, informal discussion, obtaining goods and services, information
  exchange, interviews, goal-oriented co-operation):** these are what Pedant already does, in
  role-play. Every band's tasks above are of this kind.
- **Interaction strategies:** asking for clarification, checking understanding and taking turns
  happen naturally in a conversation, if Gemma's instructions allow them.
- **Short sustained production:** describing an experience, giving information, or putting a case,
  as longer turns in a conversation.
- **Mediating communication:** Gemma can stage a situation where the learner helps two people
  understand each other, or passes on what one said, as a role-play (acting as an intermediary;
  delicate situations and disagreements).
- **Strategies:** asking for clarification and compensating happen in any conversation, if Gemma
  lets them: answering "say that again, slower" at Basic, or helping when the learner uses a word from
  their own language.

**What fits only partly:**
- **Long monologues and addressing audiences:** possible in Free talk, but long recordings cost
  memory (the speech recognizer grows with recording length), and grading a long monologue word by word
  isn't how Pedant grades.
- **Mediating a text:** relaying a text (an email, a notice) in the language. It needs a text to show
  or read out first, which Pedant doesn't do today.
- **Using telecommunications:** a phone call can be role-played (Basic: arrival times and arrangements
  with someone you know; Independent: booking, and reporting an incident), but not its audio quality.

**What doesn't fit:**
- **Formal discussion with several people:** Gemma plays one role at a time.
- **Public announcements:** nothing to interact with.
- **Reading, writing and listening to recordings:** outside a speaking app. The CEFR's levels describe
  these too, but the bands here only use the speaking scales.

**Repeat word and Repeat phrase.** In these two help modes, the learner says Gemma's translation of
what they meant, not their own wording. So the band there sets how hard the conversation and its
phrases are, not what the learner produces unaided. Only Free talk exercises the production
descriptors as the CEFR means them. The band still matters in the repeat modes: it decides the
vocabulary and structures the learner practises saying.

**Phonology.** The phonology column measures intelligibility, not the absence of an accent: from B2
up, an accent from the learner's other languages is expected and acceptable as long as it doesn't get
in the way of being understood. Pedant grades sounds against one voice's pronunciation, by design;
the bands could set how lenient that grading is (see Phonology above).

## What this means for the evals (task 5)

The qualitative features give each band things to check in Gemma's speech:

- **Basic:**
  - short sentences;
  - common words, in basic sentence patterns and set phrases;
  - concrete, everyday topics;
  - simple linking ("and", "but", "because").

  The first two are measurable without a judge (sentence length, word commonness).
- **Independent:**
  - connected sentences;
  - opinions with reasons;
  - some complex sentences;
  - handling something less routine.
- **Proficient:**
  - idiom and colloquialism;
  - connotation and implication;
  - register appropriate to the situation, kept consistent;
  - well-structured longer turns;
  - diplomacy (reservations, conditions, tact).

The Independent and Proficient features need a judge (tasks 5a and 5b).

Gemma also needs to behave like the interlocutor each band assumes:
- **Basic:** the A-level descriptors keep saying "provided the other person helps", "addressed
  clearly, slowly and directly", "the interlocutor … provides support". At Basic, Gemma should speak
  slowly and simply, repeat and rephrase when asked, and help.
- **Independent:** the learner can be asked to keep the conversation going, but Gemma may still need to
  "make an effort to support understanding" (B1 conversation).
- **Proficient:** no accommodation. Gemma speaks as it would to another proficient speaker, including
  fast, colloquial and allusive language.

An eval can check this too: whether Gemma at Basic answers a request for repetition with a simpler
rephrasing.

### By exam

**German, Goethe-Zertifikat** (official):
- **A1** (about 15 minutes, in a group):
  1. "Sich vorstellen": introduce yourself.
  2. "Um Informationen bitten und Informationen geben": ask for and give information, from word
     cards on a theme (for "Wochenende", the weekend: Sonntag, Frühstück, Sport, Familie).
  3. "Bitten formulieren und darauf reagieren": make requests and respond to them, from picture cards.
- **B1** (about 15 minutes for two candidates):
  1. "Gemeinsam etwas planen": plan something together, from four guiding points. In the model
     paper, a classmate is in hospital: when to visit, how to get there, what to bring, how to help
     once he's home.
  2. "In einem Monolog ein Thema präsentieren": a presentation from five given slides.
  3. Give each other feedback on the presentations, and ask each other a question.
- **B2:**
  1. "Vortrag halten": a prepared talk of about 4 minutes in a seminar, with an introduction, main
     part and conclusion. For "Finanzierung des Studiums" (paying for university): describe several
     options, give and weigh their advantages and disadvantages, describe one more closely. Then
     answer the others' questions.
  2. A discussion of a controversial question, arguing a position, "Argumente pro und kontra
     erörtern".
- **C1** (about 20 minutes per pair, plus preparation):
  1. A talk on a chosen topic from notes, with questions afterwards. It has to use four different
     language functions; among them: drawing attention to a problem, proposing, describing, politely
     showing understanding.
  2. "Freie Diskussion zu einer kontroversen Frage": a free discussion of a controversial question,
     from a short input text and four points.
- **C2** (about 15 minutes, 15 minutes' preparation):
  1. A five-minute talk in a seminar, weighing different viewpoints from three quotations (for "Die
     Bedeutung von Heimat", what home means), with examples, "anspruchsvolle Sprache", and your own
     position made clear; then questions.
  2. A discussion with the examiner. You pick one side of a statement, "Vertreten Sie Ihre Meinung",
     respond to their arguments, and try to convince them.

**French, DELF and DALF** (official, every level):
- **A1** (10 minutes' preparation for parts 2 and 3):
  1. "L'entretien dirigé" (about 1 minute, no preparation): the examiner asks simple questions about
     you, your family, your tastes and activities ("Comment vous appelez-vous ?", "Où est-ce que vous
     habitez ?").
  2. "L'échange d'informations" (about 2 minutes): you draw six cards, each with one word ("Prénom ?
     Téléphone ? Ordinateur ? Adresse ? Famille ? Livre ?"), and ask the examiner questions from
     them, to get to know them. You show you understood the answers ("Ah, oui ?", "D'accord").
  3. "Le dialogue simulé" (about 2 minutes): a role-play of an everyday situation. In the example
     you're the customer and the examiner the shop assistant: ask the price of what you want to buy,
     the quantities, and pay, with pictures of coins and notes, greeting and using polite forms.
- **A2** (10 minutes' preparation for parts 2 and 3):
  1. "L'entretien dirigé" (about 1 minute 30, no preparation): introduce yourself, your family,
     friends, studies and tastes; the examiner asks follow-up questions ("Qu'est-ce que vous aimez
     faire de votre temps libre ?").
  2. "Le monologue suivi" (about 2 minutes): talk on a chosen theme, answering its questions; then
     the examiner's questions. You present an event, an activity, a project or a place, linking ideas
     with "et", "mais" and "parce que".
  3. "L'exercice en interaction" (3–4 minutes): a role-play to resolve an everyday situation (in the
     example, you're a customer and the examiner works at the station). You ask for information,
     make proposals, and use polite forms.
- **B1:**
  1. "L'entretien dirigé" (2–3 minutes, no preparation): talk about yourself, your family, work or
     studies, likes, habits, past, present and plans; the examiner asks follow-up questions ("What
     did you do last weekend?").
  2. "L'exercice en interaction" (3–4 minutes, no preparation): a role-play of "une situation
     inhabituelle de la vie quotidienne", an unusual everyday situation. You give your opinion and
     propose solutions, express your feelings, insist, and check information. You use "tu" or "vous"
     according to the examiner's role.
  3. "L'expression d'un point de vue" (5–7 minutes, 10 minutes' preparation): a structured
     three-minute talk giving your opinion on the theme of a short document, then the examiner's
     questions.
- **B2** (30 minutes' preparation):
  1. "Le monologue suivi : défense d'un point de vue argumenté" (5–7 minutes): find the issue the
     document raises, announce a plan, and present and defend your view with relevant examples.
  2. "L'exercice en interaction : débat" (10–13 minutes, no preparation): the examiner questions what
     you said, and you defend your view to convince them. You confirm and nuance your opinion, concede
     points, and agree or disagree ("Je comprends ce que vous dites mais…").
- **C1** (one hour's preparation):
  1. "Exposé" (8–10 minutes): a structured talk on a theme, built from a set of documents, with three
     or four main points. The documents are a source: "En aucun cas vous ne devez vous limiter à un
     simple compte rendu des documents" (you must never limit yourself to a summary of the
     documents); you add your own comments, ideas and examples.
  2. "Entretien" (15–20 minutes, no preparation): the jury asks about your talk and discusses it with
     you; you defend your opinion and keep the discussion going.
- **C2** (30 minutes, one hour's preparation after two listenings):
  1. A summary of an audio document ("compte rendu du contenu d'un document sonore").
  2. A personal argued development of the issue it raises.
  3. A debate with the jury.

**Spanish, DELE** (secondary):
- **A1:** a short personal presentation, a talk on a topic, a conversation about it, and a simulated
  dialogue.
- **A2:** a short monologue, a description, and a simulated dialogue.
- **B1** (15 minutes, 15 minutes' preparation):
  1. A short presentation.
  2. A conversation about it.
  3. Describe a photograph and talk about it.
  4. A simulated situation, 2–3 minutes.
- **B2** (20 minutes, 20 minutes' preparation):
  1. Weighing up proposals, then a conversation.
  2. An imagined situation from photographs.
  3. A proposal with conversation.

**Turkish, TYS** (secondary): two parts: a conversation of several questions on one topic, and
independent speaking, answering one question at length.

**Russian, TORFL** (secondary): dialogues at the lower levels; at the third level (C1) a public
monologue and a public interview.

**Indonesian, UKBI** (secondary): an oral presentation, sometimes recorded into a microphone; guides
also list describing a picture, retelling, and role-play.

### The shapes, by level

The progression matches the Companion Volume's:
- **Basic:** talk about yourself and get things done in predictable situations.
- **Independent:** handle something unexpected, plan, and give opinions with reasons.
- **Proficient:** argue, persuade, and pass on and judge what sources say.

### Generalised: the task shapes for every language

The official French and German exams, every level of both, confirm the pattern the secondary sources
showed for Spanish, Turkish, Russian and Indonesian. So Pedant uses one set of task shapes per band,
for every language, without consulting each language's own exam. Only two things differ by language:
which test the band corresponds to (task 4), and the setting each scene takes where the language is
spoken (task 10).

**Basic (A1–A2):**
- **Interview about you:** Gemma asks about the learner, their family, tastes, activities and town,
  and follows up.
- **Getting to know Gemma:** the learner asks the questions, from a few prompt words (name, family,
  work, weekend), and shows they understood the answers.
- **Everyday transaction:** buy or order something, ask prices and quantities, pay; ask the way;
  buy a ticket.
- **Requests:** ask for something and respond to requests.
- **Resolve a routine situation:** a simple everyday problem (a wrong ticket, a missing item), asking
  for information and making proposals.
- **Short talk on a familiar theme:** about two minutes on an event, an activity, a plan or a place,
  then Gemma's questions.
- **Plan something together:** when and where to meet, what to do.

**Independent (B1–B2):**
- **Interview about you, with past, present and plans:** Gemma asks follow-up questions that need
  details and reasons.
- **Unusual everyday situation:** a role-play with a twist. The learner explains the problem, proposes
  solutions, gives opinions and feelings, insists, and uses the register the role calls for.
- **Plan and negotiate together:** several points to settle, making and answering suggestions.
- **Presentation on a theme, then questions.**
- **Opinion from a short text:** find the issue a short text raises and give a structured opinion,
  then answer Gemma's questions (B1).
- **Defend a view, then debate:** argue a position with examples, then defend it as Gemma pushes
  back, conceding and nuancing (B2).
- **Prepared talk weighing options:** advantages and disadvantages of several options, then
  questions (B2).
- **Describe a picture, then talk about it.**

**Proficient (C1–C2):**
- **Talk from several sources with your own reflection, then discussion:** not a summary; the
  learner's own argument, drawing on the sources (C1).
- **Controversial question:** take a side, answer Gemma's counter-arguments, and try to convince
  Gemma (C1–C2).
- **Weigh viewpoints:** a talk weighing several stated positions, with your own made clear, then
  questions (C2).
- **Report on an audio source, develop it, debate it** (C2).
- **Plain exchanges of facts are not Proficient tasks** (see the Companion Volume above).

These shapes are what the scenario ingredient lists (plan task 9) draw on, alongside the can-do tasks
from task 1. Which ones need something new in the app is below.

### Which shapes can be instructions to Gemma

**Instructions alone** (Gemma plays its part; the app works as now):
- **Interview about yourself:** Gemma is the interviewer and asks follow-up questions. This is close
  to "Gemma asks for the scene" on the roadmap.
- **Information exchange, requests, and simulated everyday dialogue:** what today's scenarios
  already are.
- **Unusual everyday situation:** a generated scenario with a twist, which the roadmap's ingredient
  lists already have. DELF B1's criteria (propose solutions, insist, express feelings, tu or vous by
  role) say what the learner's side should include.
- **Planning together:** Gemma as the friend or colleague, with a few points to settle.
- **Discussion or debate:** Gemma takes the other side, responds to the learner's arguments, and
  tries to convince them, as in Goethe C2.
- **Questions after a talk:** Gemma asks about what the learner said.

**Needs something new in the app:**
- **Prompts on screen:** word cards (Goethe A1), guiding points (Goethe B1, C1), a statement to take
  a side on (Goethe C2). A small card above the conversation would do.
- **Pictures:** describing a photograph (DELE, UKBI). Gemma 4 can read images, but the app shows none,
  and the pictures would have to come from somewhere.
- **A source to pass on:**
  - **Text:** a short text shown on screen, for an opinion on a document (DELF B1–B2) or a report on
    documents (DALF C1). Gemma could write it.
  - **Audio:** a short recording to summarise, as in DALF C2. Piper could read out a text Gemma
    wrote, which would make an audio source possible offline.
- **Preparation time:** the exams give 10 to 60 minutes to prepare a talk. The app could offer time
  to think and make notes before a talk.
- **Long turns:** talks of 3 to 5 minutes. Free talk could take them, but long recordings cost memory
  (the speech recognizer grows with recording length), so a talk might need taking in parts.

### What the exams grade that matters for Pedant

DELF B1's own grading criteria include:
- **Phonology:** "je fais les liaisons" (I make the liaisons) and "Mon accent ne gêne pas la
  compréhension" (my accent doesn't get in the way of understanding). This is the Companion Volume's
  intelligibility standard, with liaison named as something graded at B1.
- **Register at B1:** "J'utilise « tu » ou « vous » selon le rôle de l'examinateur" (I use tu or vous
  according to the examiner's role). The exam expects a choice of register at B1, where the Companion
  Volume's register scale still says "neutral register". This bears on whether Formal / Casual
  belongs at the Basic band.
- **Strategies:** its advice to candidates is the strategy scales in practice. If you don't know a
  word, find another word or explain it another way; if you don't understand, ask the examiner to
  repeat or speak more slowly.
