# Research: the bands

Research for [the levels and scenarios plan](../plans/levels-and-scenarios.md). This is task 1, the
band descriptors. Tasks 2–4 (oral exam task shapes, example courses, test labels) will add their own
sections.

## Sources, and a caveat

The source is the CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe, 2020), which updated the 2001 CEFR's
descriptors and added scales for mediation and online interaction.

**The descriptors below are paraphrased, not quoted.** The Council of Europe's PDF couldn't be
downloaded for this research, because its site (rm.coe.int) isn't reachable from the session that
wrote this. The wording here comes from the 2001 CEFR and Companion Volume scales as known, checked
against search results. Check it against the official PDF before using any wording in the app or the
evals:
https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4

The Companion Volume also adds a Pre-A1 level, below the bands, and replaces "native speaker" with
"user of the language". Neither changes anything here.

## The scales that concern Pedant

The Companion Volume describes each level with many scales. The ones about speaking are these.

**Oral production** (one person speaking at length):
- Overall oral production
- Sustained monologue: describing experience
- Sustained monologue: giving information
- Sustained monologue: putting a case (for example in a debate)
- Addressing audiences
- Public announcements

**Oral interaction** (two or more people talking):
- Overall oral interaction
- Understanding an interlocutor
- Conversation
- Informal discussion (with friends)
- Formal discussion (meetings)
- Goal-oriented co-operation (doing something together)
- Obtaining goods and services
- Information exchange
- Interviewing and being interviewed
- Using telecommunications

**Interaction strategies:** taking the floor, co-operating, and asking for clarification.

**Mediation:** passing meaning between people or texts. It has three groups of scales: mediating a
text, mediating concepts and mediating communication.

**Qualitative aspects of spoken language:** the CEFR also describes how a speaker at each level
sounds, in five aspects: range, accuracy, fluency, interaction and coherence. These matter twice for
Pedant. They describe how Gemma should speak at each band, and what the band evals (task 5) can
measure.

## The bands

Each band is two CEFR levels.

### Basic (A1–A2)

**What a speaker can do** (paraphrased):
- **A1:**
  - Use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases for concrete needs.
  - Introduce themselves and others.
  - Ask and answer questions about personal details: where they live, people they know, things they
    have.
  - Interact simply, if the other person talks slowly and clearly and is ready to help.
- **A2:**
  - Handle simple, routine tasks that need a simple, direct exchange of information on familiar
    matters: personal and family information, shopping, the local area, work.
  - Describe in simple terms their background, immediate surroundings and immediate needs.
  - Tell a story or describe something as a simple list of points.

**How they sound** (range, accuracy, fluency, interaction, coherence):
- **Range:** a basic set of words and memorised phrases; simple sentence patterns.
- **Accuracy:** some simple structures, with basic mistakes.
- **Fluency:** very short utterances, with pauses, false starts and reformulation.
- **Interaction:** relies on the other person repeating, rephrasing and helping. At A2, they can
  answer questions and respond to simple statements, but rarely keep a conversation going on their
  own.
- **Coherence:** words and groups of words linked with "and", "but" and "because".

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Greet, introduce yourself, say goodbye.
- Answer and ask questions about yourself, your family, your home and your work.
- Order food or drink; buy something; ask the price; pay.
- Ask for and follow simple directions.
- Make simple arrangements: when and where to meet.
- Say what you like and don't like.
- Describe your day, your town, a person, in a few simple sentences.
- Tell what happened, as a simple list of events.
- Ask someone to repeat or speak more slowly.

### Independent (B1–B2)

**What a speaker can do** (paraphrased):
- **B1:**
  - Deal with most situations likely to come up while travelling where the language is spoken.
  - Enter unprepared into conversation on familiar topics: family, hobbies, work, travel, current
    events.
  - Describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes and ambitions.
  - Give brief reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.
  - Tell a story or the plot of a book or film, and give reactions.
- **B2:**
  - Talk with enough fluency and spontaneity that regular interaction with users of the language
    works without strain for either side.
  - Take an active part in discussion in familiar contexts, explaining and supporting their views.
  - Explain a viewpoint on a topical issue, with the advantages and disadvantages of the options.
  - Develop a clear argument, supporting their points with reasons and relevant examples.
  - Handle a problem or a complaint, for example a disputed bill or poor service.

**How they sound:**
- **Range:**
  - **B1:** enough language to get by on everyday topics, with some hesitation and talking around
    missing words.
  - **B2:** enough range to describe clearly and give views without much obvious searching for words,
    using some complex sentences.
- **Accuracy:**
  - **B1:** reasonably accurate in familiar, predictable situations.
  - **B2:** good control, with few mistakes that cause misunderstanding.
- **Fluency:**
  - **B1:** keeps going, with noticeable pauses to plan grammar and words.
  - **B2:** a fairly even tempo, with few long pauses.
- **Interaction:**
  - **B1:** starts, keeps up and ends a simple face-to-face conversation on familiar topics.
  - **B2:** takes turns appropriately, helps a discussion along, checks understanding.
- **Coherence:**
  - **B1:** links shorter points into a connected sequence.
  - **B2:** uses a limited range of linking words to join ideas into clear, connected speech.

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Tell a story about something that happened to you, with how you felt about it.
- Describe your plans, hopes and ambitions, with reasons.
- Give your opinion on a familiar topic and explain why.
- Deal with an unexpected problem while travelling: a lost booking, a missed train, a wrong order.
- Make a complaint and get it resolved politely.
- Compare options and recommend one (where to eat, which trip to take).
- Agree and disagree in a discussion, giving reasons.
- Explain how to do something, or how something works, step by step.
- Ask follow-up questions, check you've understood, and keep the conversation going.

### Proficient (C1–C2)

**What a speaker can do** (paraphrased):
- **C1:**
  - Express themselves fluently and spontaneously, almost effortlessly.
  - Use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic and work purposes.
  - Give clear, detailed, well-structured descriptions and presentations of complex subjects.
  - Understand implicit meaning, attitudes and implications.
  - Handle delicate situations and disagreements with diplomacy.
- **C2:**
  - Express themselves very fluently and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning, even on
    complex subjects.
  - Summarise information from different sources, reconstructing arguments coherently.
  - Have a good command of idioms and colloquialisms, and an awareness of what words imply.
  - Get around a difficulty so smoothly that the other person hardly notices.

**How they sound:**
- **Range:**
  - **C1:** a broad range, choosing a fitting formulation in the right style without having to
    restrict what they want to say.
  - **C2:** great flexibility in rephrasing to convey precise shades of meaning, give emphasis and
    remove ambiguity.
- **Accuracy:**
  - **C1:** consistently high, with rare errors.
  - **C2:** consistent control of complex language, even while attention is elsewhere.
- **Fluency:**
  - **C1:** fluent and spontaneous; only a conceptually difficult subject slows the flow.
  - **C2:** a natural, effortless flow.
- **Interaction:**
  - **C1:** chooses suitable phrases to take the floor, and relates what they say skilfully to what
    others said.
  - **C2:** joins in with fully natural turn-taking, references and allusions.
- **Coherence:** well-organised speech, with a wide range of linking and organising devices.

**Can-do tasks for a conversation:**
- Persuade someone to change their mind, without offending them.
- Smooth over a conflict, or deliver unwelcome news tactfully.
- Explain something complex (a decision, a system, a situation) clearly and in structure.
- Argue a position and respond to counter-arguments on the spot.
- Shift register: the same request to a friend, a stranger and a boss.
- Understand and use humour, irony, idiom and implication.
- Summarise and compare viewpoints in a discussion, and draw a conclusion.
- Negotiate: weigh options, make concessions, reach an agreement.

## Which descriptors Pedant can exercise

Pedant is a spoken conversation with Gemma, one turn at a time. The learner's speech is heard by a
speech recognizer and ZIPA.

**What fits:**
- **Interaction (conversation, discussion, getting goods and services, information exchange,
  interviews):** these are what Pedant already does, in role-play. Every band's tasks above are of
  this kind.
- **Interaction strategies:** asking for clarification, checking understanding and taking turns
  happen naturally in a conversation, if Gemma's instructions allow them.
- **Short sustained production:** describing an experience, telling a story, explaining or putting a
  case all fit as longer turns in a conversation.
- **Mediating communication:** Gemma can stage a situation where the learner helps two people
  understand each other, or passes on what one said, as a role-play.

**What fits only partly:**
- **Long monologues and addressing audiences:** possible in Free talk, but long recordings cost
  memory (the speech recognizer grows with recording length), and grading a long monologue
  word by word isn't how Pedant grades.
- **Mediating a text:** relaying a text (an email, a notice) in the language. It needs a text to show
  or read out first, which Pedant doesn't do today.
- **Using telecommunications:** a phone call can be role-played, but not its audio quality.

**What doesn't fit:**
- **Formal discussion with several people:** Gemma plays one role at a time.
- **Public announcements:** nothing to interact with.
- **Reading, writing and listening to recordings:** outside a speaking app. The CEFR's levels describe
  these too, but the bands here only use the speaking scales.

**Repeat word and Repeat phrase.** In these two help modes, the learner says Gemma's translation of
what they meant, not their own wording. So the band there sets how hard the conversation and its
phrases are, not what the learner produces unaided. Only Free talk exercises the production
descriptors as the CEFR means them. The band still matters in the repeat modes: it decides the
vocabulary and structures the learner practises saying.

## What this means for the evals (task 5)

The qualitative aspects give each band things to check in Gemma's speech:

- **Basic:**
  - short sentences;
  - common words;
  - concrete, everyday topics;
  - simple linking ("and", "but", "because").

  The first two are measurable without a judge (sentence length, word commonness).
- **Independent:**
  - connected sentences;
  - opinions with reasons;
  - some complex sentences;
  - handling something unexpected.
- **Proficient:**
  - idiom and colloquialism;
  - implication;
  - register shifts;
  - well-organised longer turns;
  - diplomacy.

The Independent and Proficient features need a judge (tasks 5a and 5b).
